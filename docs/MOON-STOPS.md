# A small moon with six worthwhile stops

Proposal for the next build. These new content areas and games are not implemented by the audio update. The existing Workshop, Charging Station and About panel remain the starting points.

The visitor should understand what Nikhil makes within 20 seconds, then have a reason to stay. Use six primary destinations, with a small optional discovery on the far side. Give each landmark a distinctive silhouette, color accent and short name; keep the lunar surface spacious.

| Stop | What the visitor finds | Landmark and placement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project Hangar | Three to five selected projects; problem, contribution, outcome, demo and source where available | Expand the existing mint workshop near spawn. Each featured project gets a physical display bay. |
| Mission Control | About, experience and a short personal story, presented as a readable timeline | A low observatory with a warm window, close to the name monument. A telescope can point toward Earth. |
| Résumé Pod | A readable résumé plus a clearly labeled PDF download | A compact boarding-pass kiosk between the hangar and Mission Control; no puzzle or game required to reach it. |
| Lunar Arcade | Playable Snake first, then Snakes & Ladders, with explicit start/exit controls and short sessions | A coral dome on the next quarter of the moon. Rover movement pauses while a game has keyboard focus. |
| Experiment Lab | Small prototypes, creative coding, unfinished experiments and the existing visitor signal | Extend the charging station. A rotating experiment gives repeat visitors something new. |
| Signal Tower | Contact, GitHub, LinkedIn and availability only when confirmed | A slim antenna visible above the horizon. Contact also remains in direct navigation. |

## Make the whole moon easy to navigate

- Add a compact **Map / M** control showing the globe, six labels and the visitor’s position. Choosing a stop offers a route beacon or direct travel.
- Put Work, Story and Résumé within a short drive of spawn. Place Arcade and Lab farther apart so travel feels intentional. Keep Contact accessible from the menu at all times.
- On approach, illuminate the landmark and show one clear action, such as **E · Open projects**. Mirror the action with a touch button.
- Mark visited stops with small passport stamps. They are optional mementos; portfolio content never requires collecting them.
- Keep a simple, keyboard-accessible portfolio alongside the world. Games have their own pause/reset/exit controls, and leaving a game restores focus and rover controls.

## Details worth adding later

A far-side lookout with a photo mode and collectible lunar postcard would reward exploration without adding another large building. A small field-notes shelf can live inside Mission Control once real writing exists. UFO compliments should remain playful fictional visitor chatter, not presented as testimonials.

## Build order

1. The map, route beacons and six landmark footprints.
2. Real projects, story, résumé and contact content.
3. Snake, then Snakes & Ladders; isolated game input and persistent local best scores.
4. Experiment rotation, photo mode and optional stamps.

This avoids turning the moon into a crowded link directory. The professional content stays easy to find, while the arcade, experiments and far-side discovery make exploration worthwhile.
